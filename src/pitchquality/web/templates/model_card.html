Model card

An expected-whiff model for pitch evaluation

The model estimates the probability that a swing misses from the physical properties of the pitch. It was trained on 2025 Statcast and evaluated on a stretch of 2026 it never saw. The aim is to separate stuff, which travels with the pitcher, from results, which also carry the hitters he faced, where he located, and luck.

The fix that mattered

The first version of the stuff model scored {{ k.stuff_auc_before_vaa }} AUC and lost the predictive-validity test: a pitcher's own past whiff rate forecast his future whiff rate better than the model did. That is the right reason to reject a model, so the question became what it could not see.

The answer was approach angle. Statcast publishes trajectory as position, velocity, and acceleration 50 feet from the plate, so the angle the hitter sees has to be integrated forward. A flat vertical approach angle at the top of the zone misses bats that the same velocity and spin would not miss on a steeper plane. Adding vertical and horizontal approach angle moved the stuff model from {{ k.stuff_auc_before_vaa }} to {{ k.stuff_auc }} AUC.

The saved model behind the leaderboard

The live pipeline fit the same two models once on the same 2025 window, saved them, and scores every swing with that saved copy. It never refits. These AUCs come from that fit and are read from the database.

How the stuff score is built

Each swing's stuff-model probability is rescaled so that 100 is the mean and 10 points is one standard deviation of the 2025 training swings{% if live.stuff_reference_mean and live.stuff_reference_std %} (mean {{ live.stuff_reference_mean | rate }}, standard deviation {{ live.stuff_reference_std | rate }}){% endif %}. Those two reference values are frozen with the model, so a score means the same thing in every refresh. A pitcher's leaderboard score is the mean over all scored swings. Averages of many pitches bunch together, so pitchers land in a narrow band around 100. The Stuff+ index in the research report is scaled across pitcher averages instead and spreads much wider. The two are not interchangeable.

Expected whiff on the leaderboard is the full model's mean. Whiff over expected is actual minus that. Ranks start at {{ qualified_min }} swings, the floor the research report uses. The database itself keeps every pitcher.

Does the grade predict anything?

The 2026 test window is split at its median date. Each first-half number is used to forecast second-half whiff rate for the same {{ card.predictive_validity[0].n_pitchers }} pitchers. The model here is the stuff model.
